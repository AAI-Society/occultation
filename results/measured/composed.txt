      The pooled presentation here is a crude estimate, not a measurement, and it is almost certainly too low: it implies a 99% reduction where the desk study's own pooled figure implies 80%. It also excludes the escrow tag, which is modelled; the desk study attributes 4.6 ms (33%) of presentation to it.

Measured and published figures differ: the composed path is 14x cheaper
here than the desk study's figures imply. The composition error is unaffected —
it is an error in arithmetic, not in hardware. Composed BBS+ costs 51x an
Ed25519 sign-and-verify round trip of 0.03 ms here.

The divergence is not uniform. Cached pairings are the one optimization measured
on both sides: the desk study reports them saving 74% of verification, and they
save 11% here. So the measured block does not corroborate the desk study's
optimization structure either, which is a second finding rather than noise.
